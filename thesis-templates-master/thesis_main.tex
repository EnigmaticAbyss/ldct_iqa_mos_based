\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Noise and artifact patterns in low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) can affect diagnostic confidence and motivate radiologist-aligned image quality assessment. This work investigates mean opinion score (MOS)-based LDCT image quality assessment using MedGemma, a medical vision-language model (VLM). We fine-tune MedGemma and compare supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with group relative policy optimization (GRPO), considering GRPO initialized from either the base VLM or an SFT adapter. The results show that SFT alone is the most effective strategy under the evaluated settings. GRPO performs better when initialized from an SFT adapter than from the base VLM; however, the additional refinement stage does not surpass SFT alone.\footnote{Code: \url{https://github.com/EnigmaticAbyss/ldct_iqa_mos_based}}

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} low-dose CT, image quality assessment, MOS prediction, MedGemma, vision-language model, supervised fine-tuning, group relative policy optimization
